Why this is hard

A larger chemical input with faster decay looks much like a smaller input with slower decay at a downstream gauge. Per reach that ambiguity is exact. Everything on the following slides is about how much structure—parameter reduction, priors, gauge design—it takes to break it, and what that costs.

The model

The snapshot operator, and how it is solved.

This is the algebra of your five-page attachment, coded once and differentiated automatically.

Contract

M  = X N + (I − X)
Φ  = diag( exp( −(5/3) αᵢ kᵢ ) )
Qc = (I − Φ N)⁻¹ Φ Rc  +  Φ M⁻¹ K⁻¹ ΔSc
  • Rc — local chemical input flux (mass/time). Not water runoff.
  • ΔSc — signed chemical mass anomaly relative to equilibrium. Not a time increment between two steps.
  • α — nonnegative first-order loss rate; k — wave travel time, with residence time 5k/3 as in the draft.
  • Φ — transmitted fractions, not removed fractions.
  • Qc — chemical mass discharge. Concentration needs Qc ÷ known water flux, with matching units and dry-reach masking.

The physical interpretation of the storage term—where Φ sits, the sign structure, and what ΔSc means for a real river—is still a draft reading and is flagged for your review, not asserted here.

Solved as sparse recurrences

qᵢ  = φᵢ ( Rcᵢ + Σ upstream q )
zᵢ  = ( ΔScᵢ/kᵢ − xᵢ Σ upstream z ) / (1 − xᵢ)
Qcᵢ = qᵢ + φᵢ zᵢ

Both matrix inverses are donor-before-receptor recurrences, so no dense N×N matrix is ever formed. Production evaluates them by parallel tree doubling: 771 topological levels need ten rounds, giving O(n log depth) work and the same bound on reverse-mode storage.

Reference: network, provenance and the earlier water-routing parity

Static topology from the supplied MERIT/RAPID region-74 archive (Zenodo record 20672740), verified against its published MD5 and SHA-256. The archive holds static tables and reach coordinates — no river or catchment shapefile geometry.

Region 74: 72,654 reaches, 36,404 headwaters, 109 outlets, 771 topological levels. Every experiment on this page uses the largest outlet component, 64,579 reaches upstream of COMID 74072654, which drains 2,959,707 km² against 3,325,384 km² for all components combined. That is a provisional operational domain, not a validated Mississippi study boundary.

The earlier water-routing milestone is unchanged: a five-reach Muskingum kernel reproduces a frozen MATLAB R2024b oracle to a maximum absolute discharge difference of 1.78×10⁻¹⁵, autodiff matches finite differences to 1.22×10⁻⁹ relative directional error, and a noise-free joint recovery of two runoff pulse amplitudes and a shared travel time drove held-out outlet RMSE from 2.540 to 6.56×10⁻¹⁴. That is a software and identifiability control, not a field expectation.

This JAX package is the routing and chemistry core only. The broader MATLAB research environment still owns the basin studies, the GLDAS/HYBAM/SWOT/nitrogen readers, the complete Optimal Estimation scripts and the preprocessing utilities. No full MATLAB parity is claimed.

The controlled setup

What is real, what is invented, what is held fixed.

Reading the findings correctly depends entirely on this table. Everything downstream is an OSSE: invent a truth, simulate observations, and see what the inversion recovers.

Real

  • Static MERIT/RAPID region-74 topology, checksum-verified
  • Reach connectivity, coordinates, catchment and drainage areas
  • Travel time K and weighting X from the supplied tables

Synthetic

  • Water forcing: uniform 1 mm/day runoff
  • Chemical inputs: 1 mg/L of that water
  • Decay-rate truth field: analytic, never used as a training label
  • Gauges: virtual stations chosen from metadata only

Held fixed in the fit

  • Neural experiments: chemical inputs, water, K, X; storage anomaly zero
  • Joint snapshot: everything except the five free parameters
  • Optimiser, tolerances, initialisation and seeds pre-registered before each run

Absent

  • Measured nitrogen or DOC concentrations
  • A finalised Mississippi study boundary
  • Time integration, 4D-Var or HMC — this is snapshot algebra
  • Calibrated posterior uncertainty
The neural parameterisation

Thirteen parameters: latitude and log K feed three tanh hidden units, whose output is mapped to a bounded log decay rate. It never sees a decay-rate label. Training data are concentrations at virtual gauges perturbed with 20% CV lognormal noise; the assumed fitting sigma is √(log(1+0.2²)) = 0.19804. Held-out and evaluation gauges are excluded from the objective, and the runners assert bitwise that corrupting them cannot move the fit.

Tripling gauges did the work

Holding architecture, prior, initialisation, likelihood and optimiser fixed and changing only 30 → 90 training gauges cut median relative rate error from 47.2% to 18.7% and raised correlation with the truth field from 0.37 to 0.825. That is the first fit in the series to beat the predict-the-mean level.

The mechanism is noise absorption

Per-sample misfit, where 0.5 is what a correct model reaches: the 30-gauge fit explained its own samples better than the truth does (0.350 vs 0.535), then did far worse than the truth on 60 unseen stations (0.884 vs 0.600). At 90 gauges the fit sits near the truth on both blocks (0.445 / 0.570).

Concentration skill is not rate recovery

The 30-gauge fits already beat the constant baseline on concentrations at evaluation gauges while still being worse than it on the rate field. Concentrations integrate rates along the network, so many rate fields explain the same samples.

What these rows do not show

The noise-free, stronger-prior and 90-gauge rows change different factors — they are not one controlled ladder. Each is one seed, one initialisation, one noise realisation. The 13 weights stay non-identifiable: the L2 penalty drove two hidden units to near-duplicates, an exact null direction of any gauge Jacobian that more coverage does not remove.

The snapshot did separate inputs from decay

In this five-parameter reduction the exact per-reach source/rate degeneracy survives only as a within-region correlation — 0.720 west, 0.788 east — with cross-region correlations near zero. Nothing exceeded the 0.9 flag.

The storage number is a prior statement

Its data eigenvalue is below 1, so 30 gauges carry less about it than the prior does. The 58% low bias is exactly prior shrinkage: a linear-Gaussian MAP predicts 0.5033 prior sd and the optimiser returned 0.5046. Doubling the prior width alone moves it from 0.252 to 0.448, while the other four shift by at most 0.044 prior sd.

Why storage is weak here is structural

M⁻¹ is quasi-local with a sign-alternating, geometrically damped upstream coefficient, so a distributed anomaly reaches a gauge essentially only through that gauge's own reach — while the equilibrium term integrates the whole upstream network. Storage is 4.9% of total flux at the network median but only 2.3% at these gauges.

Adding storage cost prediction

Held-out standardised log RMSE went from 0.0234 with storage fixed at the oracle value to 0.0840 with it free — while every numerical check still passed. Converging, reporting success and hitting no bound is not a recovery gate.

Reference: admissibility, storage basis, and how to read the modes

Singular vectors are mixtures of the five parameters, not named parameters. Under this gauge design the smallest mode happens to load almost entirely on storage (0.999), so naming it is defensible there. Under the alternative design on the next slide it would be wrong: the smallest mode loads 0.960 on decay-west. Ordered singular values must not be relabelled as parameters.

Storage basis. ΔSc = b · pattern with a north–south tanh pattern in latitude, chosen deliberately distinct from the west/east parameter regions and frozen before fitting. That choice favours separability; it is a modelling decision, not a claim about real anomalies.

Admissibility. The draft's positivity claim is not repeated. At truth, 32,296 of 64,579 storage contributions are negative and nothing is clipped, yet there are zero negative total fluxes. Guaranteeing nonnegative total flux while the input multiplier may fall to 0.368 caps the tested |ΔSc| at 7.7% of a reach's equilibrium chemical mass (max 281 kg). Analytic lower bound on total flux 5.2904×10⁻⁵ kg/s; worst of 32 box corners 5.3417×10⁻⁵ kg/s.

Uncertainty. H = JᴥJ + I is positive definite even where the data carry nothing. Its inverse is an approximate local linearisation, not a calibrated posterior, and no HMC was run. Observations here are noise-free; the 0.19804 sigma is an assumed fitting and metric scale, not a description of the data.

Storage improved, materially

The amplitude went from 0.2523 to 0.4259 against a truth of 0.6, its marginal variance reduction from 42.4% to 71.4%, and its sensitivity to prior width halved: doubling the prior sd now moves it 0.115 instead of 0.195. Median gauge drainage area fell from 3,466 km² to 94 km², raising storage's share of flux at the gauges from 2.39% to 5.50%.

Decay information collapsed

Decay is read from contrast built up along long flow paths, and the new design largely stopped observing them. Decay-west marginal information fell 11.7 → 1.40 and its error grew from +0.064 to +0.343 prior sd — 0.0636/day against a truth of 0.05.

Held-out prediction got worse

At the ten reused, large-drainage held-out gauges, standardised log RMSE rose 0.084 → 0.175, because the new training set no longer samples that regime. Whole-network error improved, 0.211 → 0.182. Both are reported; neither is explained away.

Read this as a trade-off

This is a redistribution of a smaller total information budget, not a free improvement. It supports this sampling choice for this control; it does not show that storage is generally observable, and the reachwise source/rate ambiguity is untouched. A prespecified mixed design that tries to hold both is running and is deliberately not reported here.

Finding 4 · misspecification

Wrong chemical inputs: decay took the blame, but it did not hide.

The 90-gauge case reproduced bit-for-bit, then refitted with one change: the fitter's forward model multiplies every local chemical input by 1.20 at all 64,579 reaches. Observations, water, K, X and the truth field are untouched — the analyst simply assumes the wrong load.

Median signed rate bias moves from −2.6% to +18.2% against an imposed +20% input bias, and the fitted-over-reference rate ratio has median 1.284. The compensation was not limited by the rate map's bounds: no reach came within 0.1% of either end.

Compensation is real, and proportionate

The learned rates absorbed the load error at roughly the size of the error itself. At 0.0628/day the biased fit's rate RMSE is worse than predicting the truth field's mean. This is genuine input/decay compensation, and it is what the experiment was built to be able to see.

But the concentration fit got worse too

So this is not the “fits concentrations fine while learning wrong decay” case. The refit removed 71.9% of the excess training misfit and 67.0% of the imposed log offset, then stopped.

The trace is in the residual mean

Mean scaled residual +0.279 sigma for the biased fit against −0.024 for both the correct fit and the truth itself. The RMS moves far less (1.132 vs 1.028) and on 90 samples would be easy to dismiss as noise — but a correctly specified fit has no reason to sit a quarter of a sigma high everywhere.

Why this is probably a favourable case

A uniform ×1.20 load is a uniform log offset, and transmission exp(−5/3 α k) varies with heterogeneous K, so the change the rates could actually deliver had half the spread of the offset it was cancelling. The L2 penalty also resisted. This run does not separate those two causes, and a spatially uniform error is exactly what a spatially varying decay field should be worst at absorbing — so detection here is likely easier than it would be in general.

Limits

What none of this establishes.

Stated plainly, so the findings can be used without being over-read.

01

Not general identifiability

Unrestricted joint source/decay inversion remains non-unique. Success in a five-parameter reduction, or with 13 weights and fixed inputs, is not evidence that the per-reach problem is solvable.

02

Not validated chemistry

Every truth field is analytic and generated by the same physics used to fit it. No measured nitrogen or DOC has entered any experiment. Real data is a later route, not something already done.

03

Not a blind test

Held-out and evaluation gauges are fixed, repeatedly used sets on the same known network. They are not independent catchments, and their scores have already informed discussion.

04

Not a robustness claim

Each headline number is one seed, one initialisation, one noise realisation, one gauge draw. A fresh-seed replication is running precisely because every conclusion currently rests on a single realisation.

05

Not calibrated uncertainty

The Gauss-Newton covariance is an approximate local linearisation, and the L2 weight penalty is a regulariser, not a prior anyone has justified physically. No HMC or full posterior was run.

06

Not a settled domain or storage physics

The 64,579-reach component is provisional, the archive contains no shapefiles, and the placement of Φ, the sign structure and the meaning of ΔSc remain open questions for you.

Deliberately not reported

Two experiments had written outputs when this page was built but no verified final report, so nothing from them is quoted here: a fresh-seed replication of the 90-gauge coverage case, and a mixed low/middle/upper-quartile gauge design for the joint snapshot. Both are the direct follow-ups that findings 1 and 3 called for.

Reference: verification and test suites

Each experiment reproduced its predecessor's saved arrays with exact equality before fitting, recorded SHA-256 manifests of every pre-existing file it depended on, and re-checked them afterwards. Designs were frozen to disk before any observation was generated.

Test counts are not additive. Different sessions ran overlapping module subsets. The largest single recorded run is 100 passed — chemistry, network, physics, parity, gradients, basin data, sparse decay, neural decay, gauge coverage and source bias. Other recorded runs include 68, 62 and 57 passed over smaller overlapping subsets. Environment: Python 3.13.13, JAX 0.11.1 on CPU with float64, NumPy 2.5.2, SciPy 1.18.1.

The curated numbers quoted on this page, with their sources and caveats, are published as results-2026-09-08.json. Raw arrays, run logs and internal reports are not published.

Decisions we need from you

Four choices, then the next experiment.

Select a path to frame the discussion. My recommendation is D1: a global input-scale parameter is the sharpest available test of whether the input/decay ambiguity is real at this coverage or merely unexploited.

Selected discussion path

D1 — Estimate a global chemical-input scale: 14 parameters instead of 13, starting from the biased inputs, and test whether the same 90 gauges separate a uniform load scale from the decay field.

Q1

Prior structure

The L2 weight penalty is a regulariser, not physics. What should actually constrain a decay field — a rate range, a spatial correlation, a CARDAMOM-derived prior?

Q2

Success criterion

Rate recovery and concentration skill disagree in these runs. Which one is the headline for RAPID-Chem?

Q3

Input uncertainty

The ×1.20 test used an invented uniform bias. What is a defensible error model for chemical inputs, and is a covariance available?

Q4

Snapshot or time

Everything here is equilibrium algebra. Is the snapshot the intended scientific object, or a stepping stone to a time-integrated 4D-Var?
